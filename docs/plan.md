# Plan

The plan agreed with the owner on 22 October 2026, extended on 25 October 2026 (steps 11–17). Each step is one pull request. Update this file when a step is done or the plan changes.

The interface must stay simple as settings are added: see "A simple interface" in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md). Every step below follows it.

## What the app does

- **Area:** the whole world, a continent, or chosen countries (one or several). Only countries and territories with official Google Street View are offered.
- **Distribution:** the visitor picks one of Vali's maps (An Arbitrary World by default, A Community World, A Balanced World, …). The app splits the requested number of locations (up to 130,000) across the countries of the chosen area in that map's proportions (largest remainder method), and shows the per-country table before the search starts. For several countries or a continent, the chosen map's proportions for just those countries are used; for a continent, Vali's own continent distribution is offered too. A single country gets the full number.
- **Criteria**, ticked separately:
  - **No road name:** none of the panorama's arrows has a label.
  - **End of coverage (dead end):** exactly one arrow. The camera faces away from the arrow, towards where the coverage stops.
  - **Photo years** (added later): the photos Google shows by default at a spot, normally the newest, are from a chosen range of years between 2009 and this year.
  - With several ticked, a location must have all of them. The export can tag each location with its country's name (ticked by default), what it has ("No road name", "Dead end") and the year of the photos; the visitor chooses.
- **Search:** random spots inside the country; the nearest official panorama within a radius (a setting); locations at least 100 m apart. A live table shows each country's target, found count and state. The visitor can stop at any time and download what was found.
- **Shortfalls:** a country stops after a set number of lookups in a row that find nothing new. At the end, the app offers to fill the gap from the other countries, keeping their proportions, or to keep what was found.
- **History** of search settings, as in Lookout. No shareable links.
- Google's official Maps JavaScript API is used for the lookups (not an undocumented endpoint).

## Vali data

Vali ([slashP/Vali](https://github.com/slashP/Vali), GPL-3.0) is the source of the map distributions and continent lists. The owner asks slashP about reuse; the data file credits Vali.

- `tools/vali.js` reads Vali's `Weights.cs` and writes `src/data/vali.json` (with the Vali commit and date). It stops instead of writing a file that looks wrong.
- The build embeds the data; the page never contacts GitHub.
- `.github/workflows/vali.yml` runs monthly (and by hand), and opens a pull request when the data changed, listing what changed. It runs the checks itself and starts the Tests workflow on its branch, as pull requests created by a workflow do not start it.
- Readable map names live in a short list of our own; a new Vali map shows its code until named.

## Steps

1. **Search one country** (done): the search loop, official coverage only, both criteria, the count, spacing, live progress, stop and download, tags. Since then: the country field suggests countries while typing, a larger "Open in Street View" button, tag options (country name, what it has, year), and the photo years criterion.
2. **Areas and distributions** (done): whole world, continents, chosen countries, the Vali import and the monthly workflow, the per-country table.
3. **Shortfalls** (done): attempt budgets (a setting, 2,000 spots by default) and the end-of-search offer.
4. **History** of search settings (done): the last 20 searches in this browser, with "Run again", "Use these settings", remove, clear, and a switch to turn it off.
5. **Following roads to the end of coverage** (removed): from a found panorama that was not an end, the search followed the road up to 150 steps to find one. It made finding ends slower rather than faster, so it was taken out again.
6. **Resuming a long search** after closing the tab (done): saved in IndexedDB while it runs; on the next visit, continue, download what was found, or discard. One unfinished search is kept; never the key.
7. **No bad camera** (done): leaves out panoramas 6656 pixels high from 2020 on, as Vali does, except in India, Nepal, Nigeria, Lebanon, and São Tomé and Príncipe; off by default. With it, "Use an older photo instead" (on by default at first, off since step 23) uses the newest photo at the spot that is not bad camera, from the list of photos Google's answers carry (`time`, undocumented: without it, nothing changes). Lebanon was added to the countries offered.
8. **Time left** (done): a forecast in the status line, learnt while the search runs: each country's spots per location so far and the spots per second. A country where locations are rare is counted with the spots it can be expected to take before it gives up, not with the many it would need to fill up (fixed after a real search of 100,000 forecast 3 days instead of about a day). Shown after 20 seconds and 50 spots, rough and smoothed.
9. **Random spots only on land** (done): country-coder's borders reach far out to sea, so most random spots in island and coastal countries landed in the water (Malta's shape is about 95% sea, Japan's 92%, Chile's 90%, New Zealand's 87%). A land map built from Natural Earth (world-atlas, land-10m) keeps spots on land; it adds about 140 KB to the page.
10. **Time spent searching** (done): a clock first in the counters, ticking every second; it adds up across "Fill the gap" and continuing a saved search, and stays after the search ends.
11. **Settings layout and help** (done). The first screen shows only what most visitors need: where, the main criteria, how many, "Find locations". Everything else moves into closed "More options" sections, one per topic: **Distribution** (which map shares the locations), **Photos** (camera, dates, bad camera, older photos), **Camera view** (from step 16), **Search** (radius, give up after) and **Map file** (map name, tags). A closed section shows a one-line summary of what is set ("Photos: Gen 4 only, May 2015 – now"), marks settings that differ from the default, and has "Reset to defaults"; the browser remembers which sections are open. Explanations move out of the page into **help dialogs**: a small "?" button next to a setting opens a dialog with the full text. Keyboard and screen readers must work for all of it.
12. **Settings as a file** (done). "Save settings to a file" and "Load settings from a file", next to "History". The file is JSON (readable in any text editor; the same format as the map files), named like `malta-dead-ends.settings.json`, with a format name and a version number. It holds every search setting, including a custom distribution (step 17), never the Google key. On loading, everything is checked like any untrusted input; a missing setting gets its default; settings the page does not know are ignored, with a note. One shared description of the settings is used by the history, the unfinished search and the file, so a new setting is never forgotten in one of them.
13. **Months in the date filter** (done). The default "from" month is January, so choosing a year starts at its beginning; the period never starts before May 2007. From and To become month and year, as Google gives each photo's date that way. The earliest choice is May 2007, when Street View started. To is "now" by default, which follows the calendar. Saved years move over (From 2015 becomes January 2015, To 2020 becomes December 2020). The year tag gets a choice: the year, or month and year as `mm-yyyy` ("05-2023").
14. **Camera generation filter** (done). Choose Gen 4, Gen 2/3 and/or bad camera, told apart by the image size Google returns: 8192 pixels high is Gen 4; 6656 is Gen 2/3, or bad camera when the photo is from 2020 on and outside India, Nepal, Nigeria, Lebanon, and São Tomé and Príncipe (the rule from step 7). Gen 2 and Gen 3 cannot be told apart, so they are one choice. **Gen 1 is left out of every search**, whatever is ticked (3328 × 1664, confirmed on real 2007–2008 panoramas; other sizes count as an unknown camera). With any camera ticked, "No bad camera" is greyed out. "Use an older photo instead" is extended: when the newest photo at a spot is not one of the chosen cameras, the newest older photo that is (and matches everything else) is used. A tag option gives each location its camera ("Gen 4", "Gen 2/3", "Bad camera").
15. **Intersections** (done). Three or more arrows. As "End of coverage" means exactly one arrow, the two become one choice: "Arrows: any, end of coverage, intersection"; "No road name" stays a separate tick box. The saved "End of coverage" moves over to the new choice. A tag option "Intersection", by the same rule as the filter. At an intersection the camera faces along a random arrow.
16. **Camera direction and tilt** (done; the owner checked in map-making.app that a positive tilt looks up). The direction relative to the car (front 0°, right 90°, back 180°, left 270°, anything in between), from the direction the car faced when the photo was taken (`centerHeading`, documented in Google's API; checked on real panoramas in October 2026: it matches one of the arrows within a few degrees, except now and then at intersections). Chosen on a top-down drawing of a car with a camera arrow to drag, with a number field and the arrow keys. The tilt from straight down (−90°) to straight up (+90°), on a side view of the car. "Random" for each: any direction; a tilt within a range set with two handles (−20° to +20° by default). By default nothing changes: along the road, and at an end of coverage facing where it stops. The map file carries both (heading and pitch).
17. **Your own distributions and presets** (done). Start from any of Vali's maps (or a preset of yours) and edit the table of locations per country. One choice above it, "Enter as: percentages / numbers" (percentages by default; agreed 25 October 2026):
    - **Percentages:** each country gets a share; "Number of locations" stays the visitor's own setting, and the shares are turned into whole numbers for it with the same method as Vali's maps (largest remainder), so they always add up to the total. The table shows the resulting number next to each share, while typing, and says when a small share rounds to 0. Decimals are allowed ("0.5" and "0,5"). Shares that do not add up to 100 are used in proportion, with a line "Adds up to 80%: shared in proportion" and a "Make it 100%" button.
    - **Numbers:** each country gets an exact number; the total is their sum, and changing the total scales them all.
    - Switching between the two converts what is there (300 / 200 / 500 ⇄ 30% / 20% / 50% of the total).
    - "Save as preset" with a name keeps it in this browser, listed under "Your maps" above Vali's, with rename and delete. A preset keeps its mode: a percentages preset scales to any total, like Vali's maps; a numbers preset gives the same counts every time. The history and the settings file keep the mode and the values.

18. **After the plan** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): a random camera direction within a range around the car (clockwise, across the front if needed; 0° to 360° by default); in your own distributions, countries a Vali map leaves out are shown with 0 (and Palestine, in none of Vali's continent lists, is offered for Asia), and the note that a small share rounds down to 0 moved into the help.

19. **Roads and arrows** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): "No road name" and the arrows moved from the first screen into their own closed section, between "Photos" and "Camera view"; a line under step 2 says what is looked for. The rule in CONTRIBUTING.md changed with it: the first screen shows where, how many and "Find locations". Also in your own distributions: "Edit the shares" scrolls to the editor every time, a × next to each share takes that country out (sets it to 0), and "Set all to 0" (after a second click) starts from nothing.

20. **Zoom** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): in "Camera view", how far the camera is zoomed in, the same for every location or random within a range (0 to 2 by default), from 0 to 4 with one decimal. The scale is that of map-making.app, GeoGuessr and Google's Street View player, checked by the owner with a test map: 0 is the widest view (−1 looks the same), 1 is about Street View's normal view, each step of 1 about twice as close, and 4 only a little closer than 3; GeoGuessr uses the zoom. By default nothing changes: zoom 0, as the map files had before. The Street View link opens with the matching field of view (180° halved for each step, within the 10° to 100° such links take). Set with a slider (two handles for the range) or by typing. No tag, at the owner's choice. Also at the owner's request: the number of locations is 300 by default (was 100).

21. **Your own distribution with equal shares** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): "Start your own (equal shares)" next to "Make your own from this map" starts "Your own" without choosing a map first: every country of the area gets the same percentage, adding up to exactly 100% (the largest share takes the rounding). Equal rather than empty, as agreed with the owner: it can be used at once, and "Set all to 0" starts from nothing in one click. Replacing unsaved edits of "Your own" asks for a second click; presets are not touched.

22. **Greyed out looks greyed out** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): link buttons that cannot be used yet ("Make your own from this map", "Start your own (equal shares)", "Edit the shares" with one country) are faded and not underlined, and say why when pointed at; tick boxes that do not apply are faded with their text. "Use an older photo instead" is no longer indented under "No bad camera", as it applies to the cameras ticked too.

23. **Older photos off by default** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): "Use an older photo instead" is no longer ticked by default. A visitor who ticked it keeps it ticked; history entries and settings files that say on stay on, and those without it read as off.

24. **Welcome dialog** (done, 26 October 2026, at the owner's request): it now also tells about your own distributions (from a map, or from scratch with equal shares or all at 0; percentages or numbers; presets), the settings file (save, load, share; never the key), the camera choice and "Camera view".

25. **Country tags from the owner's list** (done, 28 October 2026, at the owner's request): with the country tag on, each location gets the tag from the owner's list of 198 countries, kept in `src/data/country-tags.json` and built into the page. Most tags are the usual English name; "USA" and "UAE" are shortened. Before, the tag was the browser's English name for the country, which differed between browsers and read "Hong Kong SAR China", "Türkiye" or "Bosnia & Herzegovina". Territories not on the list (Gibraltar, Puerto Rico, Réunion and others) keep their English name.

Later, to be discussed when they come up: more tag options.

## Known limits

- Vali's data has two slips (August 2026): An Arbitrary Rural World lists Bermuda as 0 and as 1 (1 is used), and An Improved World lists Costa Rica as 200 and as 20 (200 is used, as Vali does where it picks countries from a list). The update pull requests report such slips.
- A few countries in Vali's maps have no official coverage (Belarus, Pakistan and others, about 0.01% of a map); they are left out.

- France's borders include Clipperton Island, which the borders count as France itself; a few spots land there.
- The older photos come from a list that Google's official API carries but does not document (`time`); Google could change or remove it. The app reads it defensively, and without it simply leaves bad camera out.
- Trekker coverage (taken on foot) is not filtered out: Google's official API does not say which camera took a panorama. Indirect signs (closer spacing between panoramas, image size) were discussed and parked for now (October 2026).
